# Jallikattu Protests in Tamil Nadu

The Jallikattu protests were significant demonstrations that took place in Tamil Nadu, India, beginning in January 2017 [2, 3]. These protests were sparked by a 2014 Supreme Court ban on Jallikattu, a traditional bull-taming sport considered an integral part of Tamil cultural heritage [3, 5].

## Background and Causes
Jallikattu became a symbol of Tamil pride and cultural identity [5]. When the Supreme Court upheld the ban in 2014, it led to widespread discontent among Tamil people who viewed the ban as an attack on their traditions [3]. The protests gained momentum in early 2017 when thousands of people, largely organized through social media, began gathering at Marina Beach in Chennai to demand the legalization of Jallikattu [2, 4].

## Timeline and Scale
The first large protests occurred on January 8, 2017, at Marina Beach in Chennai [2]. By January 18, 2017, at least 4,000 people were camping at the protest site [4]. The protests continued for an extended period, with thousands of people remaining at Marina Beach [6].

## Police Response and Findings
According to a 2025 report by Justice S. Rajeswaran, the police response to the protests was justified [1]. The commission report stated that "undesirable elements" infiltrated what began as peaceful protests, leading to violence that necessitated police intervention at Marina Beach and other locations [1].

## Cultural Significance
The protests highlighted the deep connection between Jallikattu and Tamil identity, as evidenced by how the movement demonstrated how cultural traditions can become powerful symbols of regional pride and political mobilization in India [5].
