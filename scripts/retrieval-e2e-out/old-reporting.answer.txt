# The Jallikattu Protests in Tamil Nadu

The Jallikattu protests were a series of demonstrations that took place primarily in January 2017 in the Indian state of Tamil Nadu. These protests were centered around the traditional bull-taming sport called Jallikattu, which had been banned by the Supreme Court of India.

## Background and Trigger
The protests erupted after the Supreme Court upheld a ban on Jallikattu in 2015, citing animal welfare concerns. The ban was challenged by the Tamil Nadu government, but when their efforts to revive the sport through an ordinance were met with skepticism, large-scale protests began [4].

## The Protests
- The demonstrations began in January 2017 and quickly gained momentum across Tamil Nadu [4].
- Protesters included students, software professionals, and ordinary citizens who saw the ban as an attack on Tamil culture and tradition [4].
- Notably, the protests were characterized by their peaceful nature initially, with people of all ages participating, including senior citizens, women, and children [3].
- The protests continued regardless of weather conditions, with demonstrators "brav[ing] scorching sun and rain" [3].

## Government Response
- On January 19, 2017, the central government issued an ordinance to allow Jallikattu to be conducted [1][4].
- However, protesters continued their demonstrations, demanding a permanent solution rather than a temporary ordinance [3].

## Escalation
- The protests eventually turned violent in some parts of Tamil Nadu, with reports of protesters torching vehicles near police stations [2].
- The violence broke out particularly in Chennai and other parts of the state [2].

## Significance
The Jallikattu protests represented a powerful display of cultural identity and grassroots mobilization in Tamil Nadu. They highlighted the tension between traditional practices and modern animal welfare concerns, as well as the ability of ordinary citizens to organize large-scale demonstrations in the digital age.

These events are now considered part of the background of Tamil Nadu's political and cultural landscape, though they occurred several years ago and are not recent developments [5].
